  Each language also gets a constructor and test-constructor row, and a stream line saying when the operation runs and when the streaming feature's items are read.
  
  Three kinds of check enforce these:
  - **Text invariants.** A declaration whose own text shows `throws`, `static`, `def self.` or `@classmethod` must be read that way, whatever the forms say.
  - **Per-forms pins.** `failure` and `expect` hold what was read off the runtime, so a changed runtime fails the reader test even in an unported language.
  - **Guard checks for providers:**
    - `fails`: the signature carries the marker, and the printed call carries the call word (go `err`, c `&err`, rust `unwrap`, zig `.err`; swift `try` is declared but untested, since swift has no provider).
    - `on`: a member declared on the type cannot be listed among instance members, and constructor and test-constructor calls must be made on what the runtime declares.
    - `field`: a field is not declared as called, and the reverse.
    - `streamItems`: a stream kind whose sentence says items arrive as iterated must match what the code does.
